RADSIM

A physics-based medical imaging simulator

Hi! Welcome to RadSim.

This program simulates real x-ray interactions behind realistic user interfaces. I wanted access to a software suite like this while going through a Radiation Technology program, but the product either did not exist or was prohibitively expensive. Here you can test adaptations, get familiar with the concepts, and play with the effects of ionising radiation without any of it landing on a patient.

Everything you image is a real voxel volume segmented from CT data, and the picture is made by casting rays through it. Nothing is drawn or faked: change the kV and the contrast changes because the photoelectric effect falls away, not because a slider told it to.

Some housekeeping

  • This project was built substantially with AI-assisted coding (Claude Code). Getting it to where it is now would have taken me years; instead it took days. Some features may not be fully functional because the AI believed they were working correctly. Take the simulations with a grain of salt.
  • Everything runs in your browser. The images are computed on your own device, so a phone or an older laptop will be slower than a desktop. The CT contrast injector plays a fixed preset timeline you can watch but not reprogram.
  • The EI and DI values can be finicky. With no real machine to benchmark against they do not carry much weight. Manual chest technique reads about right; lateral chest, hand and knee still read a few DI high, and I am fairly sure that is the technique chart against the phantom rather than the exposure index itself. Still working on it.
  • Some of these interfaces are reconstructed from my own memory of using them. I have no recorded video or photographs to work from, so there will be inaccuracies. If you see me in person, tell me what I got wrong.
  • This is a teaching tool, not a clinical one. Nothing here should be used to set real technique, estimate real dose, or make any decision about a real patient.

If you are new, start with a mode's Tutorial button rather than Open. It walks the whole workflow control by control and explains what each one does to the beam and to the image.

Enjoy. Working on this has ironed out a few of my own misunderstandings, so hopefully it does the same for you.
Matt

X-ray
Projection radiography with AEC, grids and technique
Position the patient at the table, the wall stand or a free detector, set the technique or let the AEC meter it, and expose. Polyenergetic ray casting through voxel anatomy, with scatter, grids, beam hardening and an exposure index.
CT
Scouts, scan planning, acquisition and reconstruction
Zero the table, take scouts, plan scan groups and recons, then watch the gantry acquire. Filtered back-projection with contrast timing and bolus tracking on the console.
IV Placement
Peripheral IV placement for contrast
A closed-system catheter in the antecubital fossa, the radial forearm or the back of the hand. Feel for the vein, prep the site, insert at the right angle, lower on the flash and thread, then clamp, dress, flush and cap. Guided, practice and assessment modes.
Fluoroscopy
Live pulsed fluoroscopy on a C-arm
Pulsed screening on a GE OEC C-arm: hold the pedal, choose a pulse rate and see dose and noise trade off in real time. Last-image hold, a beam-on timer and a C-arm you can orbit around the patient.
Bone densitometry
Dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry
A scan at two energies separates bone from soft tissue: the soft tissue cancels and what is left is the areal bone mineral density, written up in a densitometry report.
Mammography
Low-energy breast imaging at 26 to 32 kV
Target and filter combinations, compression, an AEC tuned for a low-energy beam, and CC and MLO views of a high-resolution breast phantom.
Ultrasound
B-mode, M-mode, colour and spectral Doppler
Pulse-echo imaging through the same voxel anatomy, with speckle, acoustic shadowing and post-cystic enhancement coming from the scanline physics. A beating heart for M-mode, and colour and pulsed-wave Doppler of pulsatile flow.
Model Editor
Paint voxel phantoms slice by slice
Build or alter a phantom: paint materials onto transverse slices with a live 3D preview, then save it as a subject the scanners can image.
Desktop only: slice painting needs a wide screen and a fine pointer.
Realistic X-ray
Experimental: full-resolution radiography on the GPU engine
The X-ray mode with the most realistic images: full DR matrices computed by the Python GPU backend. Needs RadSim running locally with the backend; disabled on the website.
Desktop only
Realistic CT
Experimental: full-physics CT on the GPU engine
The CT mode with the most realistic images: the fine-pitch detector, every physics feature and full reconstruction, computed by the Python GPU backend. Needs RadSim running locally with the backend; disabled on the website.

    Motion & M-mode
    M cursor where the line cuts

    The anatomy moves, not the hand: the probe holds its seat and the patient breathes and beats underneath it — which is why the speckle rides along with the tissue instead of sitting still in the screen. M-mode plots the one cursor line against time, six seconds a screen, one gridline a second. Count the gridlines between beats and you have measured the rate off the image.

    —
    Colour is the component of flow along the beam, so a vessel crossed at 90° shows nothing however fast it runs — rock the probe (or steer a linear array) and it appears. Red toward, blue away, as the bar says. Drop the scale and the fast middle of a vessel wraps to the opposite colour, bright to bright with no black between: that is aliasing, the same phase wrap as anywhere else. Depth caps the scale (PRF ≤ c/2d). Colour gain decides how much power counts as flow — too high and noise speckles the box; the wall filter removes slow shifts — too low and the moving liver flashes, too high and slow veins vanish.

    The foot brace turns the leg in 15–25°, which lays the femoral neck flat under the arm. Without it the neck turns toward the beam, projects shorter and reads denser — about 3 % higher with the leg left neutral, far more turned out — so a positioning change between visits can look like a change in the bone.
    The T-score compares this patient with a young adult of the same sex; the Z-score compares them with someone their own age. That is why an 80-year-old can be osteoporotic by T and entirely unremarkable by Z — and why a low Z is the one that says something else is going on.
